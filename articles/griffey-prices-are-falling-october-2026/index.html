Hold Up — 10 Ken Griffey Jr. Cards That Just Sold for Less Than Last Time

October 9, 2026 · 5 min read · real eBay sold prices

Before you panic-sell everything: the Griffey market is not collapsing. The Card Ladder Griffey index (119 cards) is up 151% in the last year, 95% in six months, 40% in three. What changed is the last two weeks. For the first time in about a year the line stopped going straight up, and a handful of cards that had gotten overheated sold for less than their prior sale.

So here are ten of them. Ranked by dollars lost from the previous sale of the same card in the same grade, not by percentage. Every number is a real eBay sale.

10. 1990 Leaf #245 — PSA 10

$380 on eBay auction, 30 bids (Oct 4) · prior sale $426 (Sep 27) · down $46 (11%)

Second-year base card. Every 1990 base card had been going up extremely fast, so a one-week pullback on 30 bids reads more like a breather than a problem. (It went back up to $420 three weeks later.)

9. 1997 Topps Gallery Peter Max #PM5 — PSA 10

$718 on eBay auction (Oct 4) · prior sale $800 (Aug 20) · down $82 (10%)

One of the artist cards in Topps Gallery — Peter Max did the artwork on both the front and the back. Clean copy, no obvious reason for the dip beyond the gap between sales.

→ 1997 Topps prices

8. 1996 Flair Diamond Cuts #5 — PSA 9

$630 on eBay auction, 22 bids (Oct 2) · prior sale $725 (Sep 22) · down $95 (13%)

You're reading the listing right: the seller used the back of the card as the first photo. The front, buried as picture two, looks great. It was also the seller's first sale. Everybody starts somewhere, but a back-of-card lead photo on a $700 card is probably part of why this went for less.

→ 1996 Flair prices

7. 1992 Studio #232 — PSA 10

$710 on eBay auction, 23 bids (Oct 4) · prior sale $850 best offer (Aug 20) · down $140 (16%)

The bubble-gum Studio card — another base card made famous by the photo. PSA 10 population is only 116, which is why a base card from 1992 is a $700–$850 card at all.

→ 1992 Studio prices

6. 1992 Donruss Elite Series #13 — PSA 9

$820 on eBay auction, 25 bids (Oct 4) · prior sale $1,003 (Sep 23) · down $183 (18%)

Numbered to 10,000 on the back, one of the first serially numbered cards ever. Two sales eleven days apart, $183 apart. (It recovered to $998 by the end of October — so this was the dip, not the trend.)

→ 1992 Donruss prices

5. 1997 Topps Hobby Masters #HM1 — PSA 10

$1,200 on eBay auction, 39 bids (Oct 3) · prior sale $1,420 (Oct 1) · down $220 (15%)

Not numbered, but it has a refractor-style shine. Two PSA 10s sold two days apart and the second one went for $220 less on 39 bids. That's the clearest "overheated" example on this list — plenty of bidders, just not at the old price.

→ 1997 Topps prices

4. 1998 Topps Gallery of Heroes #GH1 — PSA 10

$2,627 on eBay auction, 29 bids (Oct 4) · prior sale $3,000 best offer (Sep 21) · down $373 (12%)

Die-cut stained-glass card, super clean. I went looking for a flaw that would scare bidders off and didn't really find one — maybe a mark or two on the slab, nothing on the card. This one is just the market.

→ 1998 Topps prices

3. 1997 Topps Gallery of Heroes #GH4 — PSA 9

$840 best offer (Oct 7) · prior sale $1,254 on eBay auction (Sep 6) · down $414 (33%)

Total PSA population 163: 96 PSA 9s, 53 PSA 10s. The photo on this listing is noticeably dark. The September sale was from Goldin, and their photo had the stained glass lit up so the card popped. Same card, same grade, $414 difference, and I think most of it is presentation.

→ 1997 Topps prices

2. 1997 Bowman's Best International Refractor #BBI2 — PSA 9

$1,375 on eBay auction (Oct 2) · prior sale $1,825 (Aug 11) · down $450 (25%)

Another presentation problem. First photo is off-center, second photo has a huge glare across the card, and the back photo is so bad you can't see Griffey's face. When a buyer can't assess the condition of a $1,500 refractor, they bid like it's a $1,300 one.

→ 1997 Bowman's Best prices

1. 1999 Topps Chrome Lords of the Diamond Refractor #LD1 — PSA 10

$5,750 on eBay auction (Oct 3) · prior sale $8,000 best offer (Aug 20) · down $2,250 (28%)

The biggest dollar drop, and I think I know why. I've owned one of these and made a video on it. This copy is sitting all the way at the bottom of the slab — one of the "floating" holders with no bottom border — so the card has slid down and you can't see the bottom corners at all. When I buy a PSA 10 I want to look at the corners and decide for myself whether it's a 10. Here you can't. The consignor should have pushed the card back up before photographing it.

(And the market agreed it was a one-off: the next PSA 10 sold for $7,500 on October 29.)

→ 1999 Topps Chrome prices

What this list says about the market

The drops ran from 10% to 33%, or $46 to $2,250. Four of the ten listings had something wrong with the presentation — a back-of-card lead photo, a dark shot, glare, a card slumped in its slab — and those four include the three biggest dollar drops on the list. Three of the ten (Leaf, Elite Series, Lords of the Diamond) had already sold for more again by the end of October.

That's the data. My opinion on top of it: if you're selling a Griffey over $500, lead with the front, light it, and make sure the corners are visible.
